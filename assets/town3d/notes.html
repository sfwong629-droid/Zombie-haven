What's real and what's a stand-in. The survivors are the male base template: the same rigged 3D model and animations as the sprites, now playing live. The Medical Tent is the Hunyuan model from the building test. The cabins, watchtower, palisade, garden, campfire, lamps, trees and rocks are quick shapes built in code, in the mockup's layout and colours, to stand in until each gets its own ChatGPT → Hunyuan model. The zombies are a placeholder: the survivor model at a quarter of the detail, tinted green, with a shambling walk.

Survivors walk freely anywhere inside the walls, finding a way around buildings, the garden and the fire, then stop to idle, cheer, punch, swing or fire, depending on what they carry.

Map. The game's own grid (28 × 30 tiles of 1.3 units, the same scale as the sprites) with its road layout, the starting town area in dirt and the wall ring one tile outside it. Gates sit where the roads leave town. Tile grid shows the tiles.

Camera. Orthographic at the exact 2:1 tile angle. ⟲/⟳ (or Q/E) turn it 90° at a time. Lower front walls drops the palisade sides facing you to stubs so you can see behind them (off by default). Free camera lets you orbit anywhere for a closer look; Home snaps back.

Phone test. Stats show frame rate, screen resolution, draw calls and triangles. Drag the Zombies slider up to see how many the phone can take. Speed-ups in this version: survivors use a 14k-triangle model and zombies a 6k one (the 40k model is only for close-ups); building and tree shadows are drawn once instead of every frame, and people use soft blob shadows; anyone off screen is skipped when drawing and animated only a few times a second; big crowds animate at 30 fps; the resolution drops a step on its own when the frame rate sags and comes back when it recovers. Turning off shadows and outlines shows what they cost.
